# Field Operations: design direction

## Identity

A persistent, military extraction game for Android. Raid stakes come from what the operator carries in and successfully brings out. The current location is 48×48 with three buildings, each with one entrance. Insertion and extraction use outdoor floor tiles in the two-tile band along the map edge, separated by at least eight tiles. Loot markers require current player sight and cannot be revealed by camera panning. Maps are authored locations, not procedural dungeon floors. Decisions are deliberate: time advances through actions rather than reflexes.

## Raid loop

Prepare a loadout from the stash → insert at a randomized location → scout and scavenge → survive or avoid engagements → reach a randomized extraction point → bank the recovered equipment. Dying loses the deployed equipment and unextracted cargo. Future raids return to the same geography so players learn sightlines, routes, and cover positions.

The first prototype has one fixed location, random insertion/extraction/patrols/crates, local banked supply counts, and death losing cargo. Loadout selection and individual items remain to be implemented.

## Simulation contract

Exploration has no real-time updates. Reloading advances the world one tick, just like a movement step. A tile tap finds a path; each traversed tile advances the simulation one tick, including one patrol action per living enemy. Detection interrupts the path at the first contact tick. Guards that detect the player hold their position for that tick, and the camera frames and selects the detector so contact remains visible. Animation presents committed ticks without changing their costs. Ticks count movement, exploration reloads, body searches, and completed enemy turns. Combat movement and searches consume AP without advancing patrols; enemy actions occur in the enemy phase.

Each combat turn starts with four AP after the enemy phase; unused AP do not carry over. Moving a tile costs one AP; firing a single shot costs one or two AP by weapon; burst costs two AP; reloading costs two AP. The pistol holds seven rounds; rifle, shotgun, sniper, and SMG have distinct capacities, damage, accuracy falloff, range, and attack AP costs. A fresh operator starts with a pistol. Random crate guns and actual enemy guns are optional pickups that swap the equipped gun and leave the old gun in the source. Each gun uses its own ammo pool (9mm, 5.56, 12GA, 7.62, .45); crates provide seven rounds of a random type. Enemy guns have finite loaded and matching spare rounds, with weapon-specific range/accuracy/AP and damage scaled to one third for balance. Extraction preserves the gun and all ammo pools; death restores the starting pistol, while restarting discards unextracted pickups and restores the last secured gear. Enemies have two actions each. Shots use range, full-wall line-of-sight blocking, and directional low cover to derive a displayed probability. Waist-high barriers block movement but allow sight and fire. Adjacent defenders receive an automatic 25-point incoming hit-chance penalty from the covered direction; a shooter’s own cover carries no accuracy cost. Adjacent full-wall ends and doorway corners grant the same automatic defensive bonus for clear incoming shots around the opening, with automatic sight and fire from a legal adjacent open peeking tile. Both actors may peek, exposing reciprocal shots; static loot/objectives do not peek at the target endpoint. Flat walls and obstructed openings retain occlusion. Bonuses do not stack. Twelve of the sixteen crates spawn indoors, four outdoors, with three indoor and one outdoor crate per district. Defeated enemies leave searchable bodies that cost one tick to search (also one AP in combat). Low cover always has a floor gap from full walls, including diagonals. Each of the sixteen crates contains exactly one random item category (gun, ammo, health kit, bandage), hidden until pickup, with equal category odds and no guaranteed item. Health kits are carried and restore up to 25 HP only when used, capped at 100, for one exploration tick or one combat AP. Inventory shows the gun, all ammo pools, kits, and bandages and offers medical-use controls. A distant purple objective (minimum sixteen Manhattan tiles from insertion) spawns inside a building and is required before extraction. Its marker and navigation are available only within player sight (ten total Manhattan tiles). Extraction is hidden until pickup, then globally revealed. Enemies have cardinal facing with a 90-degree forward detection cone, follow their patrol movement direction, and investigate the last known player location when alerted. Killing the last engaged enemy immediately ends combat without advancing time or triggering an enemy phase. Surviving alerted enemies or guards who can see the player keep the engagement active; distant unengaged patrols do not. A fresh engagement starts with exactly four AP. When no living enemy sees the player after an enemy phase, exploration resumes; formerly alerted guards investigate their remembered last-seen position rather than tracking an unseen operator.

## Touch controls

Portrait-first layout. Tap terrain to move; tap a visible enemy to select it; use a separate Fire button to confirm a shot. Keep mode-adjusted hit odds visible before committing ammunition or AP. Body/head attacks cost one AP with damage/accuracy tradeoffs; burst costs two AP and independently rolls two to three rounds. Aim costs one AP for a 30-point bonus on every attack, survives firing and turns, and cancels on movement or incoming damage. Buttons for reloading, ending the turn, and extraction remain accessible below the map. A mouse follows exactly the same control path.

Touch dragging and mouse scrolling pan a clipped map view without advancing simulation. Center and Objective navigation buttons are available. A production map should also support zooming, a movement preview and confirmation, reachable-tile/AP overlays, and a forgiving target selection hit area. Distinct shapes and labels should supplement color markers for accessibility. Final pixel art should use original assets.

## Next increments

1. Separate the deterministic raid model from rendering; add saved raid state and stronger pathfinding/AI checks.
2. Add movement previews, facing-aware cover indicators, explicit enemy awareness, and feedback for each enemy action.
3. Implement stash inventory, deployable equipment, weapon/ammo definitions, armor, and extraction conditions.
4. Expand the authored map with terrain types, door interactions, and randomized loot tables.
5. Validate real Android devices and browser persistence; add original sprites, sound, and animation.

Player health now includes a non-stacking bleeding status: a damaging hit has a 15% chance to start it, and bleeding costs three HP per exploration tick or completed combat round. Combat movement, reloads, searches, aiming, and shots do not apply separate bleeding charges. Idle time is frozen. Healing cannot remove bleeding. A carried bandage stops it without healing, for one exploration tick or one combat AP. Each raid starts with one bandage; crates and corpses provide more. Weapon pickups, typed ammo, enemy loadouts, and persistent extraction gear are implemented. Multiple carried guns, armor, and a larger stash economy remain future extensions.

The expanded location is 48×48 (four times the former area), with four connected districts, 12 buildings, full-wall obstacles, and separated low cover throughout. Twenty-four enemies are randomized with six initial spawns per district, retaining the original encounter density and five-tile insertion safety distance. Loot scales to sixteen crates, four per district. Only visible map tiles are rendered, so drawing cost follows the mobile viewport rather than total map area.

Movement is presented with synchronized 0.09-second tile interpolation: each exploration step updates the world once, then the operator and all moving patrols animate concurrently. Enemy turns preserve intermediate tile positions and animate actors together. Animation advances no extra simulation ticks, and gameplay actions are blocked during motion. Detection, loot-choice interruptions, and AP limits retain their original behavior. New Raid cancels motion safely.

Sight and detection extend to ten Manhattan tiles. Pistol/rifle/sniper/SMG reach ten tiles; shotgun reaches six. Body accuracy declines by 5/4/2/6 points per tile respectively, and shotgun declines by ten, with cover and attack modifiers retained. Pointer-anchored mouse-wheel zoom and center-anchored touch buttons range from 50% to 150%; input, clipping, camera bounds, animation, and contact framing use the zoomed viewport. Zooming does not advance time.

Guard AI now separates confirmed contact from sound investigation. Player and enemy shots broadcast a twelve-tile Manhattan noise event without requiring a clear sight ray. Non-engaged listeners pathfind to the latest sound, scan four headings nearby, then resume patrol; a 32-action timeout handles blocked destinations. Patrols use randomized three-to-six-step straight legs with forward preference, brief pauses, and no unnecessary reversal. Silent rear takedowns require an unaware target and no current observer outside combat; they cost one tick, emit no noise, and preserve corpse equipment.

Player firearm attacks now prepare a real-time shot-timing overlay while the tactical world remains frozen. A moving cursor and randomized narrow green band reward accurate timing with +20 percentage points, neutral yellow preserves the base chance, and red applies −25. Final odds remain clamped to 5%–95%. Speed, band width/location, and direction vary each attack. Committing spends normal AP/ammo once; one modifier applies to every independently rolled burst round. Enemy attacks retain normal weapon probabilities.
